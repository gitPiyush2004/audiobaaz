# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Indian audiophiles, 18-45, mostly male, gear obsessives who currently buy and sell on Head-Fi, WhatsApp groups, and local classifieds. They know their gear deeply but have been burned by bad-faith sellers and phantom listings. When they arrive at this page they are either a potential seller evaluating whether the platform is credible, or a potential buyer registering curiosity. Both are skeptical. Both need to feel they found their people.

Secondary: casual curious visitors who have heard about secondhand audiophile gear but never participated. Lower knowledge, lower urgency. The page should not be written for them, but should not alienate them.

## Product Purpose

Audiobaaz is India's first managed secondhand marketplace for headphones, IEMs, speakers, DACs, amplifiers, and cables. Every listing is condition-graded by the team. Every transaction is held in escrow. Disputes are arbitrated.

The launch page exists for one job: make India's audiophile community believe this is real, credible, and worth waiting for, and convert that belief into an email signup or community join. It is not a product tour. It is a first impression that earns trust.

## Brand Personality

Precise. Obsessive. Earned.

Not "clean and modern." Not "premium consumer." This is a marketplace built by people who care too much about gear, for people who care too much about gear. The voice is direct and specific. The design should feel like it was made by someone who can hear the difference between cables.

## Anti-references

- Quikr / OLX: unstructured classifieds, no trust signals, feels dangerous
- Generic Indian startup landing pages: overcrowded, too many value props, bright CTAs, stock photography
- SSENSE / Grailed imitations: the editorial-magazine aesthetic lane is fully saturated by 2026; starting there is the first-order reflex
- SaaS-purple gradient pages: wrong register entirely
- Head-Fi forum design: functional but dated, not a credibility signal

## Design Principles

1. The page itself is the proof of quality. If it looks made by someone who doesn't care, it undermines the promise of a platform built by people who do.
2. Community-coded, not marketer-coded. Audiophiles spot inauthenticity faster than any audience. No buzzwords, no generic trust signals, no copy that could be lifted from any marketplace.
3. Precision over charm. Every element earns its place. No decorative text strips, no section eyebrows as scaffolding, no stats that aren't load-bearing.
4. The vinyl and the waveform ARE the identity. The brand's physical symbol is audio equipment. The visual language should feel like you are looking at sound, not at a software product.
5. India context is real but not decorative. The audience is Indian but the design should not gesture at India through color or pattern. The India-ness lives in the copy (escrow, UPI, courier) not the palette.

## Accessibility and Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. India has heavy mobile usage (70%+ of traffic expected on mobile). Every interaction that requires pointer precision must have a touch alternative. Animations behind prefers-reduced-motion. Font sizes must be readable without zoom on mid-range Android devices.
